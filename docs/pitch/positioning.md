# Positioning and pitch

The deck is [deck.html](deck.html). Open it in a browser. Use the arrow keys to move, F for fullscreen, N to switch between the names below, and T for a 3 minute timer.

## What it is

One credit balance that works across every AI app in the ecosystem. People top up once and spend anywhere. Apps add usage billing in about 20 lines and are paid in USDT for what their users spend. Every credit is backed 1:1 by USDT in an onchain vault.

## Category

| | Category | Read it as | Speaks to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Lead** | **Open-loop credit network** | Visa for AI credits. Today every app's credits are closed-loop, like a store gift card. We make them work everywhere. | Investors, partners, judges |
| 2 | **Universal AI wallet** | One wallet for every AI app. Email sign-in, one balance, a limit per app, revoke any time. | Users |
| 3 | **Coalition ecosystem** | ClassPass for AI apps. Independent apps share one pool of paying users, and each is paid per use. | Builders |

It is the opposite of an aggregator. Aggregators pull many models into one app they own. We leave every app independent and share the wallet between them.

Same product, three doors: lead with 1 on stage, 2 on the consumer site, 3 in the developer docs.

## Elevator pitch

Every AI app sells its own credits, so people end up with a dozen small balances stuck in apps they no longer use, and every new app has to win a fresh card form before anyone tries it. Juice is one credit balance that works across an ecosystem of AI apps. People sign in with email, top up once, and spend in any partner app, each within a limit they approve. Apps add usage billing in about 20 lines, keep their own pricing, and are paid in USDT for exactly what their users spend. Every credit is backed 1:1 by USDT in an onchain vault, so no app, and not even us, can overcharge or print credits. We earn a small network fee on every charge, and every app that joins makes every balance worth more.

## Self-critique

What the first draft got wrong, and what changed.

1. **It led with the chain.** People don't buy "onchain". They buy "it works everywhere" and "nobody can overcharge me". The chain now shows up as proof on the trust and traction slides, not in the headline.
2. **It called the product an aggregator.** Aggregators own the app and the user. We own neither. Reframed as a network and a coalition.
3. **It used "UPI for AI".** That lands in Mumbai, but UPI is free to merchants and we charge a fee, so the comparison breaks on the business model. Replaced with Visa for the model and ClassPass for the user experience.
4. **It had no answer to cold start.** A shared balance is worthless with one app. Added the wedge: apps integrate on day one for usage billing and stay for the network. Light mode lets big apps join without repricing.
5. **It had invented market sizes.** Removed. The deck only uses numbers the product can prove today.
6. **It suggested Mana.** Dropped, for the reasons above.
7. **"Next billion" was only a slogan.** It now rests on real behaviour: most of the next billion already pay through small prepaid recharges. Small top-ups that work in many apps fit that habit.

## Questions to expect

- **Why would a big AI company join?** It doesn't have to reprice. In light mode it keeps its own credits and accepts ours as a top-up.
- **Why onchain?** Limits, holds and backing are enforced by the contract, even against our own operator key. A database can only promise them.
- **Isn't this stored value, and regulated?** Yes. It runs on testnet today. Production needs a prepaid licence or a licensed partner in each market. Users can't cash out, which keeps it close to a gift card.
- **What stops a drain?** Each app is capped by a limit the user signed. The relayer has daily and per-app budgets and a gas ceiling, and it simulates every call before sending it.
- **How do you make money?** A network fee on every charge, set per app (5% in the demo), taken from the app's share.
